01 / Energy researchcontradicted

Capacity is not generation

Check a headline statistic before it enters an energy briefing.

Submitted claim

IRENA's Renewable Capacity Statistics 2024 shows that renewables generated 86% of global electricity in 2023.

What SAVER returned

The attributed statistic is misread. IRENA's Renewable Capacity Statistics 2024 is a capacity publication, and its 86% figure refers to renewables' share of global power capacity additions in 2023, not electricity generated. The same IRENA publication says renewables were 43%/43.2% of global installed power capacity at the end of 2023. IRENA's separate Renewable Energy Statistics 2024 treats electricity production in GWh and its world renewable electricity production share table runs through 2022, showing 29.1% in 2022 rather than any 86% generation figure for 2023. Independent global electricity-generation sources report about 30% renewable generation in 2023, not 86%.

Inspect source-linked findings
  1. contradicts

    IRENA's Renewable Capacity Statistics 2024 foreword says the publication illustrates growth in new installed power generation capacity in 2023; it states that by the end of 2023 renewables accounted for 43% of global installed power capacity, and that the 86% figure was renewables' share of global power additions after adding 473 GW of renewable capacity. [s1]

  2. contradicts

    The IRENA press release cited in the briefing likewise says Renewable Capacity Statistics 2024 showed total renewable capacity of 3,870 GW globally in 2023 and that renewables accounted for 86% of capacity additions; it does not describe the 86% as electricity generated. [s2]

  3. contradicts

    In Renewable Capacity Statistics 2024, IRENA's table for 'Renewable energy share of electricity capacity' lists the World row as 43.2% for 2023, confirming the publication is reporting installed electricity capacity shares, not generated electricity shares. [s1]

  4. contradicts

    IRENA's separate Renewable Energy Statistics 2024 explains that generation data are presented in GWh and includes an indicator for 'Renewable energy share of electricity production.' Its world production-share table covers 2014-2022 and reports 29.1% for 2022, not an 86% global generation share for 2023. [s3]

  5. contradicts

    Ember's Global Electricity Review 2024 reports that renewables reached 30% of global electricity generation in 2023; it further breaks out hydro at 14%, wind at 7.8%, solar at 5.5%, bioenergy at 2.4%, and other renewables at 0.3%. [s4]

  6. contradicts

    Our World in Data, using Ember's final 2023 global electricity generation data, says renewables produced just over 30% of the world's electricity in 2023. [s5]

02 / Market researchsupported

Sales share is not fleet share

Keep annual sales and the installed vehicle fleet separate in a market report.

Submitted claim

The IEA's Global EV Outlook 2024 reports that electric cars represented around 18% of new cars sold worldwide in 2023 and that the global electric-car stock reached about 40 million. The 18% figure is a sales share, not the share of all cars already on the road.

What SAVER returned

The claim is supported by the cited IEA Global EV Outlook 2024 page. The report says almost 14 million new electric cars were registered globally in 2023 and that this brought the total number of electric cars on roads to 40 million. In the next sentence, it states that electric cars accounted for around 18% of all cars sold in 2023. Because the 18% is explicitly described as a share of cars sold in 2023, while the 40 million figure is described as the total number on the roads, the claim correctly distinguishes sales share from stock/fleet share.

Inspect source-linked findings
  1. supports

    The IEA Global EV Outlook 2024 page says: 'Almost 14 million new electric cars were registered globally in 2023, bringing their total number on the roads to 40 million.' This supports the claim's 2023 global electric-car stock figure and identifies it as cars on the road. [s1]

  2. supports

    The same IEA page says: 'Electric cars accounted for around 18% of all cars sold in 2023, up from 14% in 2022...' This supports the claim's 18% figure and shows it is a sales share for new cars sold in 2023, not the share of all cars already on the road. [s1]

  3. context

    The IEA page labels the relevant section 'Electric car sales' and includes a chart note stating that BEV and PHEV figures include passenger cars only, providing context for the scope of the sales and stock figures. [s1]

03 / Digital marketssupported

A global average can hide the market

Check connectivity assumptions before generalising across income groups.

Submitted claim

ITU's Facts and Figures 2023 estimated that 67% of the global population used the Internet, but the corresponding figures were 93% in high-income countries and 27% in low-income countries. The global average therefore did not describe equal Internet use across these income groups.

What SAVER returned

The original ITU Facts and Figures 2023 Internet use page reports the same 2023 figures cited in the claim: approximately 67% of the world's population online, 93% Internet use in high-income countries, and 27% in low-income countries. The page explicitly frames these as income-group comparisons, with income groups based on the World Bank classification. Because the reported high-income and low-income figures differ by 66 percentage points, the claim's conclusion that the global average did not describe equal Internet use across those income groups is supported by the source.

4 linked findings1 cited sources0.99 model-assessed confidence
Inspect source-linked findings
  1. supports

    ITU Facts and Figures 2023 states that approximately 67% of the world's population, or 5.4 billion people, was online in 2023, while 33% was offline. [s1]

  2. supports

    The same ITU page states that in 2023 the share of people using the Internet in high-income countries rose to 93%. [s1]

  3. supports

    The same ITU page states that in low-income countries, 27% of the population uses the Internet, and identifies a 66 percentage-point gap between high-income and low-income countries and regions. [s1]

  4. supports

    The ITU page specifies that income groups are according to the World Bank classification, confirming that the cited high-income and low-income categories are income groups in the publication. [s1]
